How to Prepare for Your First Therapy Session

You don't need to prepare anything formal for a first therapy session — it typically involves discussing your background and what brought you in, so the therapist can suggest a way forward with you.

What actually happens first

Most first sessions focus on getting to know you: a bit of background, what's been going on recently, and what you're hoping to get out of therapy. The therapist leads the conversation, so you don't need a script.

If you want to prepare a little

Some people find it helpful to jot down a few things they'd like to mention, or a rough sense of what's brought them to therapy now. This is optional, not required.

It's normal to feel nervous

Feeling unsure or nervous before a first session is common. Therapists are used to supporting people through that first step, and sessions are generally low-pressure by design.

What to expect afterward

By the end of the first session, you and the therapist will typically have a general sense of what ongoing sessions might look like and how often to meet.
