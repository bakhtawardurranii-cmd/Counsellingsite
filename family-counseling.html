Family Counseling

Family counseling brings family members together with a trained counselor to work through communication breakdowns, ongoing conflict, or the strain of a difficult transition, in a structured and supportive setting.

Book a Consultation Read FAQs

Who family counseling may be for

  • Families experiencing frequent conflict or breakdowns in communication
  • Parents and children or siblings who feel disconnected from one another
  • Families adjusting to a major change, such as relocation, illness, or a new family structure
  • Families supporting a member going through a difficult period
  • Anyone who wants a neutral space to be heard alongside other family members

Common reasons people seek family counseling

  • Recurring arguments that don't seem to resolve
  • A breakdown in communication between generations or family members
  • Adjusting to change — a move, a new sibling, blending households, or loss
  • Supporting a family member through a mental health or life challenge
  • Wanting to strengthen how the family communicates day to day

What sessions may involve

Sessions may include the whole family or specific members, depending on what's being addressed. The counselor helps structure the conversation so each person can be heard and the family can work toward shared goals.

What to expect

The first session usually maps out each person's perspective on what's happening. From there, sessions focus on specific patterns the family wants to change.

Approaches used

Family counseling can draw on systemic and family-therapy frameworks that look at how family members interact as a whole, not just individual behavior — your counselor can explain what approach may suit your family.

Possible goals

Family counseling may help improve communication, reduce ongoing conflict, and support the family through transitions. It does not guarantee a specific outcome for any individual or relationship.

Family counseling may help improve communication, reduce ongoing conflict, and support the family through transitions. Results vary by person, and no specific outcome can be guaranteed.

When to consider family counseling

Consider reaching out if conflict at home feels constant, if a family transition is proving difficult, or if communication between family members has broken down and self-directed efforts haven't helped.

Frequently asked questions

What is family counseling?Toggle answer

Family counseling is talk therapy that involves multiple family members working with a counselor to address communication problems, conflict, or the impact of a shared challenge.

Does the whole family need to attend every session?Toggle answer

Not necessarily. Some sessions involve the whole family; others may focus on particular members, depending on what the counselor and family agree is useful.

What happens during a family counseling session?Toggle answer

Sessions typically involve structured conversation, with the counselor helping each person express their perspective and the family work toward shared, practical goals.

Can family counseling help with communication problems specifically?Toggle answer

Yes — improving communication is one of the most common goals of family counseling, alongside working through specific conflicts or transitions.

How can our family prepare for the first session?Toggle answer

It can help to think in advance about what each person hopes to get from counseling, though no specific preparation is required — the counselor will guide the conversation.
