Group Therapy

Group therapy brings together a small number of people facing similar challenges, guided by a trained facilitator, to share experiences, build support, and practice new ways of coping together.

Book a Consultation Read FAQs

Who group therapy may be for

  • People who find it helpful to know they aren't the only one facing a particular challenge
  • Anyone who benefits from hearing different perspectives, not just a therapist's
  • People working on social or interpersonal skills in a supportive setting
  • Those who want a more affordable, ongoing form of therapeutic support
  • Anyone comfortable sharing in a small, confidential group setting

Common reasons people seek group therapy

  • Wanting peer support alongside professional guidance
  • Feeling isolated in a particular struggle and wanting connection with others facing something similar
  • Wanting to practice communication or social skills in a safe setting
  • Preferring a group format over one-to-one sessions
  • Continuing support after individual therapy, in a lower-intensity format

What sessions may involve

Sessions are facilitated by a trained group leader and typically involve structured sharing, discussion of a shared theme, and space for group members to support one another.

What to expect

Before joining, most groups involve a brief individual conversation to check fit. Ongoing sessions follow group guidelines around confidentiality and respectful participation.

Approaches used

Group therapy can be organized around a shared theme (such as anxiety or grief) or run as a general process group, depending on what's offered — ask about the format of a specific group before joining.

Possible goals

Group therapy may help build a sense of connection, provide varied perspectives, and support skill-building in a lower-cost, ongoing format. Group composition and availability can vary.

Group therapy may help build a sense of connection, provide varied perspectives, and support skill-building in a lower-cost, ongoing format. Results vary by person, and no specific outcome can be guaranteed.

When to consider group therapy

Consider group therapy if you're drawn to peer support, want to work on interpersonal patterns in a live setting, or are looking for an ongoing, more affordable form of therapeutic support.

Frequently asked questions

What is group therapy?Toggle answer

Group therapy is a form of therapy where a small number of participants meet together with a trained facilitator to share experiences and work on shared or individual goals.

Who can benefit from group therapy?Toggle answer

People who value peer support, want varied perspectives, or are working on interpersonal skills often find group therapy useful, alongside or instead of individual sessions.

What happens in a group therapy session?Toggle answer

Sessions are guided by a facilitator and typically involve structured sharing, discussion of a theme relevant to the group, and mutual support among members.

How is group therapy different from individual therapy?Toggle answer

Group therapy involves multiple participants and peer interaction, while individual therapy is one-to-one with a therapist. Some people benefit from combining both.

Is what I share in group therapy kept private?Toggle answer

Group members are typically asked to agree to confidentiality guidelines as part of joining, though group confidentiality works differently from one-to-one sessions — your facilitator can explain the specifics.
