Depression Counseling

Depression counseling offers a supportive, confidential space to talk about persistent low mood, loss of interest in things you used to enjoy, fatigue, or feelings of hopelessness, and to work toward practical ways of coping.

Book a Consultation Read FAQs

Who depression counseling may be for

  • Anyone experiencing ongoing low mood that is affecting daily life
  • People who have lost interest or motivation in things they used to enjoy
  • Those noticing changes in sleep, appetite, or energy alongside low mood
  • Anyone who feels persistently overwhelmed, numb, or hopeless
  • People who want support alongside, or instead of, medical treatment for depression

Common reasons people seek depression counseling

  • Low mood that has lasted for more than a couple of weeks
  • Withdrawing from friends, family, or activities
  • Difficulty concentrating, working, or completing daily tasks
  • Persistent fatigue or changes in sleep and appetite
  • Feelings of worthlessness, guilt, or hopelessness

What sessions may involve

Sessions typically involve talking through what you've been experiencing, identifying patterns in mood and thought, and building specific, manageable strategies to try between sessions.

What to expect

An initial session focuses on understanding your history and current experience. Later sessions build a plan around your specific goals, revisited and adjusted as things change.

Approaches used

Approaches to depression counseling may draw on frameworks such as cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) or interpersonal therapy, depending on what fits your situation — your counselor can explain the reasoning behind the approach they suggest.

Possible goals

Counseling may help reduce the intensity of symptoms, build coping strategies, and support a return to activities and relationships that matter to you. It is not a substitute for emergency or medical care when that is needed.

Counseling may help reduce the intensity of symptoms, build coping strategies, and support a return to activities and relationships that matter to you. Results vary by person, and no specific outcome can be guaranteed.

When to consider depression counseling

Consider reaching out if low mood has lasted more than a couple of weeks, if it's affecting your work, relationships, or daily functioning, or if you simply want to talk to someone qualified about how you've been feeling.

Frequently asked questions

What is depression counseling?Toggle answer

Depression counseling is talk therapy focused on understanding and working through persistent low mood, loss of motivation, and related symptoms, using structured, evidence-informed approaches.

How is counseling different from psychotherapy for depression?Toggle answer

The terms overlap. In general, counseling tends to be shorter-term and focused on a specific concern, while psychotherapy can involve deeper, longer-term work — a therapist can help clarify which fits your situation.

Can counseling help if I'm already taking medication?Toggle answer

Many people combine counseling with medical treatment. A qualified professional can help assess what combination of support may be useful for you.

What happens in a first depression counseling session?Toggle answer

The first session usually focuses on understanding what you've been experiencing, your history, and what you'd like support with, so the counselor can suggest a way forward.

Is it normal to feel nervous about the first session?Toggle answer

Yes — many people feel this way. Counselors are used to supporting people through that first step, and the first session is generally low-pressure.
