Divorce Counseling vs Couples Counseling

Couples counseling generally aims to strengthen or repair a relationship, while divorce counseling supports individuals or couples through separating — including, at times, improving co-parenting communication after a decision has been made.

Different starting goals

Couples counseling usually starts from a goal of working through difficulties together as a couple. Divorce counseling starts from the reality, or likelihood, of separation, and focuses on navigating that process.

Overlap for couples who are unsure

Some couples aren't sure whether they want to stay together or separate. In that situation, sessions may involve elements of both — gaining clarity on the relationship while also preparing for either outcome.

Co-parenting focus

For couples with children, divorce counseling often includes support for communicating effectively as co-parents, separate from the emotional work of the relationship ending.

Choosing where to start

If you're unsure which fits your situation, it's reasonable to reach out and describe where things currently stand — a counselor can help suggest an appropriate starting point.
