Psychotherapy

Psychotherapy is a longer-term, in-depth form of talk therapy that explores patterns in thoughts, feelings, and relationships, helping build understanding and lasting change over time.

Book a Consultation Read FAQs

Who psychotherapy may be for

  • People wanting to explore recurring patterns in relationships or behavior
  • Anyone working through the impact of past experiences
  • People seeking deeper, ongoing self-understanding rather than a short-term fix
  • Those who have tried shorter-term counseling and want to go further
  • Anyone curious about their own patterns of thinking and relating to others

Common reasons people seek psychotherapy

  • Recurring patterns in relationships or decision-making
  • Wanting to understand the roots of current difficulties, not just manage symptoms
  • Processing past experiences that continue to affect the present
  • A general sense of being stuck, even without a single defining problem
  • Wanting ongoing, reflective support over an extended period

What sessions may involve

Sessions typically involve open, exploratory conversation guided by the therapist, often meeting regularly over a longer period than shorter-term counseling.

What to expect

Early sessions focus on building the therapeutic relationship and understanding your history. The pace and direction are shaped collaboratively between you and your therapist over time.

Approaches used

Psychotherapy encompasses several approaches — including psychodynamic, humanistic, and integrative methods — and a therapist can explain which orientation they practice and why it may suit you.

Possible goals

Psychotherapy aims to build deeper self-understanding and support meaningful, lasting change. Timelines and outcomes vary by person, and no specific result is guaranteed.

When to consider psychotherapy

Consider psychotherapy if you're looking for ongoing, in-depth work rather than a short-term fix, or if recurring patterns keep surfacing despite other efforts to address them.

Frequently asked questions

What is psychotherapy?Toggle answer

Psychotherapy is an in-depth form of talk therapy that explores patterns in thoughts, feelings, and relationships over time, often on a longer-term or open-ended basis.

How does psychotherapy work?Toggle answer

It typically involves regular sessions in which you talk openly with a therapist, who helps you notice patterns, reflect on experiences, and build understanding at your own pace.

What are the different types of psychotherapy?Toggle answer

Common approaches include psychodynamic therapy, humanistic therapy, and integrative approaches that combine multiple frameworks — a therapist can explain their specific approach.

How do I choose a psychotherapy approach?Toggle answer

It often comes down to what feels like a good fit between you and the therapist, along with the therapist's training and how they describe their way of working.

What happens in a first psychotherapy session?Toggle answer

The first session usually focuses on getting to know your history and current concerns, and discussing how ongoing sessions might work.

Is psychotherapy the same as counseling?Toggle answer

The terms overlap significantly. In general, psychotherapy can involve deeper or longer-term work, while counseling is often shorter-term and more focused — the distinction varies by practitioner.
