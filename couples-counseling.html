Couples Counseling

Couples counseling gives partners a structured, neutral space to talk through what isn't working — communication breakdowns, recurring arguments, trust issues, or feeling distant from each other — with a trained counselor guiding the conversation.

Book a Consultation Read FAQs

Who couples counseling may be for

  • Partners who keep having the same argument without resolving it
  • Couples rebuilding trust after a breach or difficult event
  • Partners who feel more like roommates than a couple
  • Couples preparing for a major life change together, such as marriage or having children
  • Anyone who wants a neutral third party to help a conversation go better than it does at home

Common reasons people seek couples counseling

  • Communication that turns into shouting, silence, or shutting down
  • Ongoing disagreements about finances, family, or parenting
  • A loss of emotional or physical closeness
  • Difficulty rebuilding trust after infidelity or a broken agreement
  • Wanting support through a transition — moving in together, marriage, a new baby

What sessions may involve

A typical session may include both partners talking with the counselor together, structured exercises to practice listening and expressing needs, and, at times, brief individual check-ins within the couple's overall plan. Sessions are collaborative — the counselor does not take sides.

What to expect

The first session usually focuses on understanding each partner's perspective and what brought the couple in. From there, sessions build toward specific, agreed goals rather than open-ended venting.

Approaches used

Approaches may draw on evidence-informed couples frameworks such as the Gottman Method or Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT), adapted to what the couple needs — a qualified therapist can explain which approach may fit your situation.

Possible goals

Couples counseling may help partners communicate more clearly, understand each other's underlying needs, and agree on practical ways to handle recurring friction. It is not a guarantee that a relationship will be saved or that any specific outcome will occur.

When to consider couples counseling

Consider reaching out if conflict feels stuck, if a difficult event has damaged trust, or if you and your partner simply want support having harder conversations before they harden into resentment.

Frequently asked questions

What is couples counseling?Toggle answer

Couples counseling is a form of talk therapy where both partners meet with a trained counselor to work through relationship difficulties, improve communication, and better understand each other's perspective.

Does couples counseling mean the relationship is failing?Toggle answer

No. Many couples attend counseling proactively — to strengthen communication, prepare for a life transition, or address a specific issue before it grows, not only as a last resort.

Do both partners have to attend every session?Toggle answer

Most sessions involve both partners together, though a counselor may occasionally recommend brief individual check-ins as part of the overall plan, depending on the situation.

How long does couples counseling take?Toggle answer

The number of sessions varies by couple and by what's being worked through. Your counselor can discuss an estimated plan after the initial consultation.

Is couples counseling confidential?Toggle answer

Sessions are treated as confidential between the counselor and the couple, within the limits that a therapist will explain during your first appointment.
